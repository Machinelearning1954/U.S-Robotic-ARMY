# PAUDC Character Bible — Key NPCs, Voice Guide & Rival Factions

> **FICTIONAL VIDEO GAME CONTENT.** This document describes entirely invented
> characters and organizations for an open-world action game set at the fictional
> Port Antonio Unified Defense Complex (PAUDC). No real people, real military units,
> real ranks-as-held-by-real-persons, real systems, or real procedures are depicted
> or implied. All names, callsigns, dialogue, and factions below are pure fiction
> created for entertainment. Companion document to `PAUDC_Base_Design.md`.

---

## 1. Casting Philosophy

PAUDC's cast follows one rule: **every named NPC should be screenshot-able.** They
need a silhouette you recognize at fifty meters, a voice you can hear reading the
subtitle, and one line players will quote in the group chat. The humor is warm, not
mean — the base laughs *with* itself. Power fantasy up top, family cookout underneath.

Ranks and titles are game-flavored fiction, used loosely for character color only.

## 2. Character Sheets

### 2.0 "The Don" — Malik Baptiste *(THE main character / player-character / protagonist)*
- **Role:** **The single playable protagonist of the whole game — the main character, full stop.** A returning son of the island who enlists at the PAUDC Recruitment Pavilion in the opening cutscene and rises from raw recruit to the name the whole harbor knows — "the Don." Everything the player does, he does; there is no other lead. **In the prototype (v0.74) the on-foot avatar is named "THE DON," and the game boots naming him as the protagonist** — the playable build and the canon agree.
- **Backstory:** Born in **Port Antonio** and raised on the harbor before shipping out young; a decorated **veteran of the 9th Littoral Squadron** (PAUDC's fictional maritime service — *not* any real-world navy) who mustered out after a tour and came home. His specialty was **field robotics and marine drone-craft** — the quiet reason the base wants him back, and a wink at the world's own name (U.S. Robotic — the "Unified Service" robotics corps of PAUDC canon). Everything about the service, ranks, and hardware is invented; no real branch, unit, rating, or operation is depicted.
- **Appearance:** Athletic, early 30s, warm dark skin, close fade and a short beard; carries himself easy but ready. Signature off-duty fit is the **black-green-and-gold flag colorway** — flag-print board shorts, tank or open linen shirt, gold rope chain, aviators pushed up. Player-customizable (the Wardrobe system, live in the prototype), but the flag look is his default poster silhouette. **Entirely an invented character — his face and name are original, not modeled on any real person.**
- **Personality:** Calm confidence over a big heart; courtesy-first, the way the whole game is. Quick with a line, slow to anger, loyal to the people who raised him. The power fantasy is competence and charm, never cruelty.
- **Voice/dialect:** Easy Portland Parish Patois, code-switches to Standard English with brass and officials; the player picks tone in a few key dialogue forks.
- **Gameplay function:** The protagonist across the whole campaign — driving, YARDCLASH, the Numbers Station mystery, naval and air missions, Turf Standing, Harbor Drop, the works. His OnWatch clout is the game's progression currency.
- **Secret/arc hook:** He came home for a reason he doesn't say out loud until Act 2 — and the Numbers Station seems to already know his name. (Ties into `PAUDC_Psych_Thriller.md`'s Static Hour.)
- **Concept art:** original renders generated via the design pipeline (Higgsfield `soul_cast`), marina-noir and flag-colorway looks.

### 2.1 Command Sergeant Major Odessa "Auntie Blades" Deveraux
- **Role:** Senior drill instructor, 1st Special Candidate School ("The Machete Course"), Zone 6.
- **Appearance:** Late 50s, compact and iron-postured; silver locs pinned under a razor-creased campaign hat; ceremonial machete (blunted prop, in fiction) worn across the back like punctuation. Mirrored sunglasses she lowers exactly once per cutscene.
- **Personality:** The meme engine of the whole game. Terrifying on the course, tender the second a recruit is genuinely hurt. Keeps a mental ledger of every soldier's mother's name and uses it as a weapon.
- **Voice/dialect:** Deep Portland Parish Patois that sharpens into parade-ground Standard English when she's *truly* angry — the code-switch IS the alarm bell.
- **Signature lines:** "Yuh tired? Tired is a *rumor*." / "Mi nuh shout. Mi *project*." / "Congratulations. Yuh madda would be — hmm — *neutral*."
- **Gameplay function:** Tutorial and training missions, Crucible timed trials, rank-up ceremonies, YARDCLASH roster fighter. Her reaction clips trend on OnWatch after player stunts.
- **Secret/arc hook:** She was the first person to ever hear the Numbers Station — decades ago, as a young signals trainee — and requested transfer away from The Ear the next morning. She has never said why. Kingfisher's mask is stitched with a pattern from her old unit scarf.

### 2.2 Lieutenant General Everton "Highmast" Solomon
- **Role:** Base commander, Unified Command Directorate ("The Vault Crowd"), Zone 1.
- **Appearance:** Tall, deliberate, salt-white beard against dark skin; dress uniform always immaculate except for battered leather sandals he swears are "regulation on my island." Walks the seawall alone at dawn.
- **Personality:** Grandfatherly gravity with a gambler's glint. Speaks slowly so people lean in. Genuinely believes the base's true weapon is Friday night at the Lagoon Club.
- **Voice/dialect:** Measured Standard English with a Jamaican melody underneath; drops into full Patois only for proverbs, which he deploys like artillery.
- **Signature lines:** "Every storm ask di same question. We are di answer." / "New puppy, old yard." (his all-purpose verdict on rookie mistakes)
- **Gameplay function:** Main-story mission giver at the ORACLE Table; presides over the Recruitment Pavilion enlistment cutscene; grants Command Rank office at endgame.
- **Secret/arc hook:** The betrayal cliffhanger in "Maroon Thunder Rolls Out" points at someone in his inner circle — and Solomon knew. He let it happen to flush out something bigger.

### 2.3 Chief Analyst Baldwin "Deacon Dial" Nettleford
- **Role:** Chief analyst, Directorate of Far Signals ("The Ears"), Zone 3.
- **Appearance:** 60s, wiry, cardigan over uniform in defiance of the heat; dome pod wallpapered with reel-to-reel tapes, conspiracy string, and a shrine of broken headphones ("the fallen").
- **Personality:** Half preacher, half pirate-radio historian. Treats frequencies like congregants. Convinced the island "talks in its sleep" and that his job is to listen politely.
- **Voice/dialect:** Slow, sermon-cadence Patois-inflected English, sprinkled with radio jargon he invents on the spot ("dat signal have a *limp*, yuh hear it?").
- **Signature lines:** "Silence is jus' a broadcast yuh not worthy of yet." / "Turn it up. No — turn *yourself* down."
- **Gameplay function:** Hub NPC for KINGFISHER Array intel unlocks and minimap upgrades; primary quest-giver for "The Numbers Station" mystery chain; decoding minigame vendor.
- **Secret/arc hook:** He has been *answering* the Numbers Station for years, one note at a time, and something has started answering back in his dead wife's favorite hymn.

### 2.4 Sistren Ivy Callender

## 3. Dialogue Voice Guide — Writing Patois with Respect

### Principles
1. **Patois is a language, not a costume.** It carries the warmest, sharpest, and most sincere lines in the game — never only the comic relief. Officers, elders, and heroes speak it with full authority.
2. **Readability over phonetic maximalism.** Use light-touch spellings players can parse at speed: *nuh, di, yuh, mi, dat, ting, haffi, inna, pon*. Avoid dense eye-dialect that turns subtitles into puzzles.
3. **Code-switching is characterization.** When and why a character shifts between Patois and Standard English says who they are: Auntie Blades switches when furious, Pierre when broken, Solomon for proverbs. Make the switch mean something.
4. **Grammar carries the flavor; don't respell every word.** "Mi soon come" reads perfectly without exotic spelling. Prefer authentic syntax and idiom over apostrophe confetti.
5. **Hire and defer.** In production: Jamaican writers punch up every script, Jamaican actors get veto power over lines, and a dialect consultant reviews all Patois subtitles. This document is scaffolding, not the final word.
6. **Military-comedy banter rides on top.** The joke engine is the collision of parade-ground formality with yard-style directness — rank meets rhythm. Never punch down at the accent itself; the accent is doing the punching.
7. **Subtitles match performance.** If the actor says "nuh," the subtitle says "nuh." No "correcting" Patois to Standard English in text.

### Example Exchanges

**1. Course intake (Auntie Blades × new recruit)**
> **RECRUIT:** Ma'am, I ran track in school—
> **AUNTIE BLADES:** Lovely. Di mud never watch track. *Position one.*

**2. The feud, live on air (Duppyproof × Pierre)**
> **DUPPYPROOF:** Sources say Public Affairs banned fun pending review—
> **PIERRE:** PAUDC neither confirms nor enjoys.
> **DUPPYPROOF:** Yuh hear dat, island? She *confirm* di second part.

**3. Dock wisdom (Chef Marlin × player, dominoes table)**
> **PLAYER:** One more game?
> **MARLIN:** Yuh money or yuh dignity, soldier. Mi kitchen nuh take *both*.

**4. Pre-flight (Colonel Palmer × nervous player)**
> **PLAYER:** And if the engine quits?
> **PALMER:** Den we glide, we land, we exaggerate. Easy ting.

**5. Intel handoff (Sistren Ivy × player)**
> **PLAYER:** How do you know the convoy route?
> **IVY:** Mi hear everyting.
> **PLAYER:** ...And?
> **IVY:** Mi repeat nutting. Coordinates inna yuh map. Walk good.

**6. Checkpoint Chicken (Drumstick × MP patrol)**
> **MP:** Samuels, this shack is a guard post.
> **DRUMSTICK:** And it never been guarded better, boss. Nobody attack a queue dis long. *Wing or thigh?*

**7. Storm prep (The Mudfish × Major Beckford)**
> **BECKFORD:** Errol! Hurricane coming and yuh moving like Sunday!
> **MUDFISH:** Storm haffi apply in writing.
> **BECKFORD:** ...Mi love yuh, but yuh frighten me.

**8. The whisper (Kingfisher × player, storyline finale — full silence, then)**
> **KINGFISHER:** Yuh finally tuned in.

## 4. Rival & Outside Factions (Brief Sheets)

These are **story tools, not fixed canon villains.** The narrative doc may rename,
replace, or promote any of them. All are original inventions.

### 4.1 The Stormjacks — *one possible antagonist syndicate*
- **Concept:** The tech-pirate collective behind the fictional "Stormjack drones" that trigger Skyfence defense events. Part scrapyard genius, part cult of the hurricane — they ride storm fronts in retrofitted trawlers, launching drone swarms from inside the weather where radar drowns.
- **Aesthetic:** Rust, tarpaulin, lightning-scorch decals; drones built from stolen parts and spite; their "colors" are storm-cloud gray and warning-buoy orange.
- **Gameplay use:** Skyfence tower-defense events, Storm Season raids, boss-rig "Weathervane" platforms hidden at sea. Deliberately generic in motive so the story doc can define (or replace) their leadership and endgame.
- **Comedy valve:** Their propaganda broadcasts keep getting remixed into dance tracks by DJ Duppyproof, which enrages them into increasingly theatrical responses.

### 4.2 The Bright Parish Combine
- **Concept:** A honey-voiced development conglomerate that wants the coastline "liberated for luxury." Lawyers by day, private security by night, cruise-brochure smiles throughout. They can't storm the base, so they besiege it with permits, billboards, buyouts, and influencer junkets.
- **Aesthetic:** Blinding white linen, seafoam branding, drone-shot promo videos of beaches they don't own yet.
- **Gameplay use:** Social-layer antagonist — OnWatch disinformation campaigns, sabotage dressed as "inspections," missions defending Palm Line families and the fishing town from soft-power pressure. Pierre's reputation missions collide with them constantly.
- **Hook:** Their charming regional director keeps trying to recruit the *player* — better pay, nicer shirts, no Auntie Blades at 5 a.m.

### 4.3 The Longline
- **Concept:** An old smuggler brotherhood that ran these waters for generations before the base arrived — and never agreed to stop. Not cartoon villains: they see themselves as the coast's true customs service. Some Ironbeach sailors have Longline cousins, which keeps every interdiction personal.
- **Aesthetic:** Quiet money — plain fishing boats with impossible engines, hand signals older than radio, ledgers kept in memory.
- **Gameplay use:** Smuggler-interdiction missions, underwater salvage rivalries (see St. Rose's scuttled wreck, 2.5), a gray-morality side chain where the player chooses between the letter of base law and the coast's older code.
- **Hook:** The Longline knew about the Numbers Station before the base existed. They call it "di Old Operator" — and they've always refused, superstitiously, to sail past its bearing at night.

By the creator's own request, the Don wears **the Founder's look**: short black
